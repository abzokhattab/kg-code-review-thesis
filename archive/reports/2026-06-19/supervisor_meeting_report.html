Highlighted in amber: the divergence / comparability filter — the safeguard against scoring review pairs that are too different to compare fairly.

Why the divergence filter matters (step 4). If the baseline review talks about, say, error handling while the KG review talks about a completely unrelated architectural concern, then "which is better" is not a meaningful question — the two are not answering the same thing, and scoring them head-to-head would inflate or distort the measured difference. The filter keeps only pairs where both reviews engage the same underlying PR concern, so every comparison is apples-to-apples. On this dataset all 40 pairs passed, which is itself evidence the modes stay on-topic.

RQ3 — human validation study (ready)

Validates whether human reviewers track the LLM-judge ranking (convergent validity).

  • Comparison: baseline vs kg under the normal prompt — i.e. it validates the pre-registered headline directly (no prompt confound).
  • Task per PR: the rater scores both reviews on the 6 criteria (F3*, F2*, T3, Q5, R1, C6), then gives an overall preference and a difficulty rating.
  • 6 PRs, deliberately balanced: 3 wins / 1 tie / 2 losses under the LLM judge — so the study can fail, which is what makes the validation meaningful.
  • Primary metric: Kendall's τ between human preference rank and LLM-judge KG-relevant delta rank.
  • Status: instrument built, verified, running locally. 1 blocker the data-collection webhook needs a 5-minute redeploy before raters can submit.
