1 · The thesis in one picture

Does giving an LLM code-reviewer structural repository context — beyond the raw diff — make its review better?

A diff shows what changed. It does not show who calls the changed function, which tests cover it, or what breaks downstream. A human opens the IDE to find that. The thesis asks: if you hand the LLM that context automatically, does the review improve? You compare four modes on the same PR:

ModeWhat the LLM is given
baselinePR title + description + diff only
kgbaseline + knowledge-graph context (callers, test files, dependent files)
ragbaseline + top-k semantically similar code chunks (embedding search)
hybridbaseline + KG + RAG

Generator: GPT-4o (T=0.4, seed=42). Judges: GPT-4o-mini + GPT-4o + Gemini 2.5 Flash, majority vote. Rubric: 25 binary criteria grounded in Bacchelli & Bird (ICSE'13), Bosu et al. (MSR'15), Sadowski et al. (ICSE-SEIP'18), DeepCRCEval, ISO/IEC 25010. thesis-context/CLAUDE.md

Why A and B are fine but C is not.

A was fixed before you saw any results — pre-registration. Unimpeachable.
B drops items for a reason that has nothing to do with which mode wins: a criterion every review passes (T1 at 98%, Q2 at 100%) or no review passes (M3 at 8%) mathematically cannot tell two modes apart, and one (T2) loads negatively on the construct. Removing zero-information items is standard Classical Test Theory (Lord & Novick 1968; Kelley 1939; Graziotin et al. 2022). KG still wins, and the effect gets a bit cleaner (0.47 → 0.58). This is written up properly in results/CRITERIA_DERIVATION.md.
C deletes 13 of 25 criteria because they did not move in your data, then reports the survivors. That is selecting on the outcome — the textbook definition of the thing examiners hunt for. It is also why the headline flipped to RAG. It is not in your pre-registration and it should be discarded.
One inconsistency to clean up (so it never confuses you again). Three of your files disagree about whether the refined subscale is 5 items {F3,F4,T3,M1,C2}, 6 items {+P1}, or 4 items {F3,F4,R2,T3}. The cleanest is the 5-item set in CRITERIA_DERIVATION.md, because every one of those 5 maps to a real KG evidence field (content validity). P1 (performance) was added empirically in REFINED_SUBSCALE_RESULTS.md but has no KG evidence field — that is the weak link. Pick the 5-item, delete the rest, and your story is airtight.

Bottom line for Section 3: Headline = 9-item, pre-registered (d_z 0.47, p 0.007). Robustness = 5-item refined (d_z ~0.58) with the CTT justification. Forget the 25-criterion "total" as a KG metric (RAG owns that), and forget the "11 active" cut entirely.

5 · What you can honestly claim

Strung together, the defensible thesis story is:

  • RQ1 (feasibility): Yes — a KG+RAG review pipeline runs across 40 PRs in 5 languages. Tree-sitter handles all 5; Joern gives precise call graphs on Java/Scala/C++, weaker on TS/Python, none on Go (5 Go PRs excluded from the Joern arm).
  • RQ2 (does context help): Yes, complementarily. KG improves KG-relevant criteria (p=0.007, d_z=0.47; Joern d_z=0.58). RAG improves total coverage (p=0.007). Hybrid improves both. The effect is gated: it shows up where the graph is populated (d_z=0.66 on KG-rich PRs) and depends on generator capability (helps mid-tier GPT-4o; saturated on Claude).
  • RQ3 (do humans agree): Pending — design is valid and balanced, data collection is blocked on a webhook redeploy.
The single sentence to keep in your head: "Structured repository context helps an LLM code-reviewer on the criteria that need structural knowledge — provided the graph has something to say and the base model has headroom to use it." Everything in §4 either supports or sharpens that sentence. None of it contradicts it.
